Definitions: Condorcet winner defeats every other candidate head-to-head by a strict majority of expressed comparisons; one may not exist. Pairwise support is the mean of each retained nominee’s support share in head-to-head comparisons against every original rival, weighted by the voters in that election; it is calculated whether or not a Condorcet winner exists. Approval leader has the highest independently generated approval count, not necessarily the most first choices. Omitted means not among the method's final nominee(s); eliminated means removed during a ballot sequence. The approval comparator is explicitly hypothetical: exactly one ≥60% approval qualifier becomes the sole nominee; otherwise the two highest-approval candidates proceed to a primary. That rule is not in the cited party bylaws. Existing rule uses smallest qualifying removal; the proposed rule uses §ii-first for 3–4 candidates. Ties are resolved by random candidate order (same order across methods in each trial). Frontiers are empirical nondominated scenario aggregates, with conditioned cases shown separately, not mathematically optimal voting rules or guarantees. A voting model cannot infer actual delegate psychology.
Sources: B2026-10 proposal · UCRP bylaws (Jan 17, 2026), §6.D.2.f and §11. Approximate behavioral model; not official party software. Offline, no dependencies or network requests.
